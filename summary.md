## TL;DR
Many open-source creators experience burnout or have their projects abandoned due to lack of recognition or reward for their hard work, despite gaining popularity. This is often followed by corporate takeovers or shutdowns, where valuable projects are either lost or forced to continue under new management. The timing of innovative technologies can also be a significant factor in their success, with some emerging too early to gain traction or being overshadowed by more popular alternatives.

## Summary

### Meteoric Rise & Burnout [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wzzh7Not8XE&t=0s)

1. OpenClaw: 0→200k GitHub stars in weeks, acquired by OpenAI
2. Emily Glay's Mutable Instruments: solo C++ audio toolkit, abandoned due to burnout
3. Faker.js: millions of weekly downloads
   1. 2022 dev deleted source, published v6.6.6 to npm in protest
   2. removed from own project, now community-run

> ⭐ Almost no open-source creators reap the rewards of their work

### Corporate Axe Falls [2:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wzzh7Not8XE&t=143s)

1. Parse: backend-as-a-service, bought by Facebook 2013 for $85M
   1. shut down 2016, code open-sourced for self-hosting
2. OpenSolaris 2005: ZFS, DTrace, containers before Docker
   1. Oracle bought Sun 2010, closed development, community forked last code

### Right Tech, Wrong Time [3:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wzzh7Not8XE&t=184s)

1. Meteor 2013: full-stack JS websockets, instant UI updates
   1. hard to scale, React/Angular killed it
   2. concepts later reborn in Next.js
2. Firefox born from Netscape's 1998 open-source Hail-Mary
   1. messy code required total rewrite
   2. IE bundled with Windows won on distribution
   3. Firefox arrived too late to save Netscape, yet revived browser competition
